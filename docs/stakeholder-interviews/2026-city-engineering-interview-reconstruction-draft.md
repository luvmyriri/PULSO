## Narrative transcript — reconstructed, non-verbatim

This section records what can be reconstructed from the project team's later account. It does not reproduce dialogue, speaker turns, sequence, tone, or direct quotations.

### A. Current process and organizational context

The project team identified three known government-side role labels: **City Engineer, Assistant City Engineer, and Administrative Division**. The city could not provide a complete organizational structure to the team. The precise reporting lines, named units, signatories, delegations, and duties of these roles were therefore not established. The project team asked for comparable city documentation to guide an initial role structure, pending confirmation with the city. **[R]**

The team described a need for a government system connecting report tracking, analytics, predictive analysis, data presentation, records, infrastructure assets, and internal management. They want the system to help with routine work through tracking, automation, AI assistance, and vision-based analysis. These are requested capabilities, not evidence that the office currently performs every process in a particular way. **[R]**

The supplied PULSO guide asks about project planning, estimates, contractor progress, funding and procurement, citizen intake, site evidence, and document handoffs. The supplied intake sheet is blank. No actual current sequence, processing time, form inventory, approving official, budget practice, or reporting cadence was recorded in either PDF. **[G/U]**

### B. Problems and operational friction

The project team reported that an existing **Assets** module contains information they consider important, but is difficult to understand. They also said the current BI dashboard and analytics presentation should be redesigned to show the most useful information for daily government work in a clear, non-technical manner. The desired experience places relevant data together and reduces the effort needed to find and act on it. **[R]**

They identified a specific report-navigation issue: the right-side panel shown after selecting a report should be collapsible; selecting the report name should open a full page with detailed information and the report's process. They want the map merged coherently into report tracking. **[R]**

The PULSO guide raises potential difficulties such as paper delays, unrecorded handoffs, mismatches between site progress and records, duplicate reports, and lost documents. The project team has **not** supplied interview answers confirming that any particular one of these occurs in the city office. They should be treated as investigation topics. **[G/U]**

### C. System requirements and desired improvements

The team requested a single, professional dashboard with the information government staff need immediately, organized around everyday decisions. They want analytics understandable to non-technical users and a detailed map used consistently across the system. Government-side map coverage should include **North and South Caloocan**. That map request does not by itself establish resident reporting eligibility, departmental jurisdiction, or an approved boundary dataset. **[R]**

The team also asked to divide and assign capabilities across roles; retain report tracking and analytics; improve record and asset tracking; automate processes; and support prediction, AI assistance, and computer vision. They raised the need to manage **posting updates and announcements**. The exact publishing authority, audiences, channels, approval stages, emergency rules, and current communications process were not recorded. **[R/U]**

The PULSO guide includes possible product ideas and numeric examples—such as a 25-metre duplicate radius, slippage percentages, retention amounts, and defects-liability periods. These are **not validated interview outcomes or authorized business rules**. Procurement and compliance rules require review by the appropriate city and legal owners before software behavior is designed around them. **[G]**

### D. Recommendations and synthesis for validation

The documentation team interprets the requested direction as a connected government workspace: staff should be able to move from a received concern to an assigned action, field evidence, inspection, decision, resident update, and asset history while retaining one traceable record. A role-aware dashboard should prioritize next actions and explain performance in plain language. This is a **proposal for stakeholder validation**, not a reported statement by an interviewee. **[I]**

The project team asked for research and proposals before coding. Its subsequent handoff package is at [government-system-proposal/README.md](../government-system-proposal/README.md). That package is separate design work and must not be confused with interview evidence. **[R]**

### 4. Unanswered questions for participant validation

1. Who attended the interview, in what capacities, and on what date? Which of the roles above are official titles versus informal functions?
2. Walk through one real recent concern from receipt to closure. Which records, handoffs, and approvals were actually used?
3. What information does the City Engineer, Assistant City Engineer, and Administrative Division need to see each morning? Which actions can each perform?
4. What does the current Assets module represent, and which parts are confusing or incomplete?
5. Which current spreadsheet, logbook, form, routing slip, or system is authoritative at each step? May the project team review a redacted example?
6. How are North and South Caloocan work areas defined? Who validates ambiguous locations and responsibility?
7. Who authorizes resident updates and public announcements? Which information must stay private, and what happens when a published notice is wrong?
8. What reliable historical data exists for forecasting or computer vision evaluation? What errors would be unacceptable?
9. Which service deadlines, procurement rules, engineering thresholds, retention practices, and emergency procedures are formally approved? Ask the responsible owners; do not infer them from guide examples.
