## A. Current process — illustrative narrative transcript

**Interviewer (sample prompt):** How is a city engineering concern received, checked, and routed today?

**Susana C. Untalan, Admin. Aide VI (simulated response):** The administrative desk receives the complaint and supporting documents. Staff check whether the submission contains enough information to identify the concern and contact the appropriate person, then record that it was received and forward it for technical review. If details are missing, the desk requests clarification. The record needs to show when a document was received, what was forwarded, and to whom.

**Engr. Errol L. Bitanga, City Engineer (simulated response):** A technical reviewer assesses whether the concern falls within the office's responsibility and whether field verification or another office's action is required. Issues should be prioritized using the nature of the concern and available evidence. A final decision should rely on verified records and accountable staff review.

**Engr. Lourdes M. Eustaquio, CGADH II (simulated response):** Technical review often needs the original location, photographs, earlier complaints, and any site findings together. When an inspection is needed, the responsible staff should receive a clear assignment and return their observations and evidence to the same record. An endorsement to another office should remain traceable so the desk can acknowledge it and follow up.

**Narrative synthesis (invented):** In this example, administrative intake leads to engineering review, possible site verification or referral, and a recorded acknowledgment or follow-up. ## B. Current problems — illustrative narrative transcript

**Interviewer (sample prompt):** Where could the process lose time or context?

**Susana C. Untalan (simulated response):** Re-entering details in separate logs and following up through calls or messages could make it hard to know the latest status. The desk would benefit from one place to see what was received, what is incomplete, what was forwarded, and which office must respond next.

**Engr. Errol L. Bitanga (simulated response):** A summary without the underlying documents or site evidence could make it difficult to judge urgency and progress. Leadership needs to see unresolved concerns and delayed actions, with direct access to the full record instead of relying on a chart alone.

**Engr. Lourdes M. Eustaquio (simulated response):** The same concern may arrive more than once, and the location may be unclear. Staff should be able to link related submissions while retaining each original report. Unknown or conflicting locations should remain visible for review instead of disappearing from the map or queue.

**Evidence boundary:** These are plausible issues for discussion, **not findings from the city interview**. No logbooks, processing-time measurements, duplicate counts, or staff observations were supplied to verify them.

## C. System requirements — illustrative narrative transcript

**Interviewer (sample prompt):** What should staff see and do in PULSO?

**Susana C. Untalan (simulated response):** The administrative view should show newly received reports, items awaiting information, endorsements awaiting acknowledgment, and notices that need preparation. Each item should have a clear next action and an owner. A report's details should be reachable from its name without losing the list position.

**Engr. Errol L. Bitanga (simulated response):** A citywide view should show cases needing assignment, high-priority issues, overdue work, work awaiting verification, and recent outcomes. North and South Caloocan should be visible through an understandable map, backed by a list and clear data definitions. The dashboard should distinguish observed results from forecasts.

**Engr. Lourdes M. Eustaquio (simulated response):** Engineers need the report, location, field photographs, inspection findings, assigned work, and related asset history in one connected path. The report side panel should collapse when the queue or map needs more space; opening the full report should show the complete process and evidence. AI may suggest categories, duplicates, summaries, or inspection priorities, but a staff member must confirm the decision.

**Interviewer (sample prompt):** How should public updates be handled?

**Susana C. Untalan (simulated response):** Staff could prepare a notice from verified work details and review its location, timing, audience, and wording before release. The system should show whether a notice is a draft, awaiting review, scheduled, published, corrected, or withdrawn. The authorized publisher and actual channels must be confirmed with the city.

**Evidence boundary:** The capabilities in this section are drawn from the project team's stated goals and shaped as sample responses. The named individuals have **not** validated them or accepted publishing authority.

## D. Recommendations — illustrative narrative transcript

**Engr. Errol L. Bitanga (simulated response):** Begin with dependable tracking, clear ownership, accessible case details, and accurate performance measures. Add predictive features only when the underlying records and historical outcomes support them.

**Engr. Lourdes M. Eustaquio (simulated response):** Keep a dated record of field observations and inspections. Make asset condition understandable and show which reports and work orders relate to each asset. Require review when AI is uncertain or source evidence is incomplete.

**Susana C. Untalan (simulated response):** Reduce repeated encoding and make handoffs easy to follow. Show meaningful status labels and delivery results so staff do not tell a resident that a message was sent when it is only queued or failed.

**Documentation team's provisional synthesis:** Pilot a role-aware Today dashboard, an integrated Reports queue/map/detail flow, connected Work and Assets records, a controlled announcement workflow, and reliable notifications. This is a product proposal for review; it is not a decision recorded in an actual meeting.
