[P52][Normal]: 1. Project Justification
[P53][Normal]: The primary objective of PULSO is to develop a real-time, citizen-centric feedback ecosystem that visualizes the "health" of city infrastructure through an interactive heat map, allowing Local Government Units (LGUs) to transition from reactive repairs to predictive maintenance. This paradigm shift is especially critical for Caloocan City North, a rapidly urbanizing territory facing engineering challenges due to high population density, irregular terrain, and extreme climate vulnerabilities like tidal and flash flooding.
[P54][Normal]: Currently, public utility management suffers from the "Silo Effect," where reports on busted streetlights and drainage failures are scattered across phone hotlines and paper logs, making it impossible for city planners to see the bigger picture. By integrating Gemini AI for automated report validation, PULSO eliminates the administrative bottleneck of manual complaint sorting. It functions as a specialized Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform that empowers citizens to act as sensor nodes with precise geo-tagging, ensuring that every data point contributes to a verifiable long-term capital improvement plan.
[P55][Normal]: 1.1 Statement of the Problem
[P56][Normal]: Despite the rapid urbanization of modern cities, local government units (LGUs) and City Engineering Offices experience critical inefficiencies in managing public infrastructure and resident reports. These difficulties are often caused by the lack of a centralized, high-precision reporting and command-and-control platform that integrates automated triage, geospatial data, and real-time status tracking. As a result, operations rely on outdated manual reporting, fragmented social media posts, and informal communication channels. This situation leads to massive report redundancy, mismanaged priorities, and a significant lack of accountability that diminishes civic trust.
[P57][Normal]: This study is limited to urban operations and infrastructure management within City Engineering Offices, focusing on the interaction between residents and local government responders. It centers on the development of a professional-grade platform designed to categorize, cluster, and monitor public utility issues such as electrical hazards, road damage, and waste management. Residents with general inquiries unrelated to physical infrastructure or those residing outside the specific LGU jurisdiction are not included in the scope of this study.
[P58][Normal]: This study seeks to answer the following questions:
[P59][Normal]: How does the Visibility Gap and the reliance on delayed manual reports affect the ability of City Engineering Offices to maintain a real-time overview of infrastructure status?
[P60][Normal]: In what ways does Report Redundancy—specifically receiving hundreds of duplicate complaints for a single issue—overwhelm LGU help desks and hinder operational efficiency?
[P61][Normal]: How does Triage Inefficiency impact the response time for high-priority hazards when they are buried under low-priority, unsorted reports?
[P62][Normal]: What are the consequences of the Lack of Accountability regarding citizen updates, and how does this contribute to growing civic apathy?
[P63][Normal]: Why is Social Media Governance (e.g., using Facebook for official reports) unsuitable for professional work order lifecycles compared to structured, geospatial data systems?
[P64][Normal]: How can the integration of AI-driven triage and deduplication algorithms improve the accuracy and speed of urban service delivery?
[P65][Normal]: 1.2 Proposed Solution
[P66][Normal]: To address the identified gaps in urban operational efficiency, this project proposes the development of PULSO, a centralized, professional-grade command-and-control platform. The proposed system aims to bridge the gap between resident feedback and LGU action through the following solutions:
[P67][Normal]: Gemini Vision Triage: An AI-driven module utilizing the Gemini model to process multimodal inputs (images and text), automatically categorizing issues and assigning a "Severity Score" (1–10) to ensure high-priority hazards are addressed first.
[P68][Normal]: Deduplication Engine: A geospatial algorithm based on DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering) and the Haversine formula to group nearby reports into a single "Incident Ticket," preventing database clutter and redundant workloads.
[P69][Normal]: Operational Heat Map: A live, color-coded dashboard that visualizes problem clusters (Red for Critical, Green for Resolved), allowing the LGU to prioritize repairs based on geographic density and urgency.
[P70][Normal]: Automated Status Loop: A transparent notification system that informs citizens at every stage of the resolution process (Received → Dispatched → Resolved) to restore public trust.
[P71][Normal]: Structured Data Management: A robust database-driven approach that replaces "algorithmic noise" with precise geospatial data, ensuring a professional work order lifecycle from report to resolution.

[P102][Normal]: Research Design and Methodology: The Waterfall Model
[P104][Normal]: This study strictly employs the Waterfall Model as its primary Systems Development Life Cycle (SDLC) methodology. The Waterfall Model was strategically selected due to the rigorous regulatory, legal, and operational constraints inherent in developing civic technology for Local Government Units (LGUs). Because systems dealing with public infrastructure and citizen data must comply with strict legislative frameworks—such as the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) and municipal procurement standards—a highly structured, sequential, and linear approach is mandatory. The Waterfall Model ensures that each distinct phase of the project is exhaustively researched, fully completed, rigorously documented, and officially approved by stakeholders before any transition to the subsequent phase occurs. This mitigates the risk of scope creep and ensures that the final deliverable aligns perfectly with the initial bureaucratic requirements. The methodology is structured into the following six highly detailed sequential phases:
[P105][Normal]: 1. Requirement Phase (Data Gathering & Feasibility Analysis): This foundational phase involves comprehensive data gathering to establish both the functional needs of the citizens and the technical constraints of the LGU. The research team conducts extensive community surveys via Google Forms, alongside semi-structured interviews with domain experts, including City Engineering dispatchers and LGU administrators. The objective is to empirically document existing systemic bottlenecks, such as the "Visibility Gap," the prevalence of duplicate reporting, and the frustrations stemming from location ambiguity. By mathematically quantifying these pain points, the researchers generate a definitive list of Functional and Non-Functional Requirements. These requirements dictate the absolute minimum viable product parameters, such as the necessity for a 5-meter GPS accuracy radius and the need for an automated status loop to combat civic apathy.
[P106][Normal]: 2. System Design Phase (Architectural Planning & Modeling): Once the requirements are locked, the project transitions into the System Design Phase, which serves as the technical blueprint for the entire ecosystem. In this phase, the abstract requirements are transformed into concrete computational logic and visual models. This involves the meticulous formulation of the mathematical algorithms that will power the platform, primarily the Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (DBSCAN) logic for geospatial deduplication and the Haversine formula for calculating spherical Earth distances. Furthermore, this phase encompasses the design of the Gemini 2.5 Flash AI multimodal triage schemas, ensuring that AI responses are strictly confined to predefined, machine-readable JSON outputs. The team also develops comprehensive Data Flow Diagrams (DFDs), Entity-Relationship Diagrams (ERDs) for the MySQL geodatabase operating on SRID 4326 standards, and high-fidelity User Interface (UI) mockups for both the citizen mobile application and the LGU web dashboard.
[P107][Normal]: 3. Development Phase (Coding & Construction): The Development Phase marks the physical construction of the system, where the blueprints generated in the previous phase are translated into executable source code. This phase adopts a highly modular programming approach, utilizing a specialized technology stack tailored for high-concurrency environments. The frontend mobile client, designed for citizen reporters, is programmed using Flutter to ensure seamless cross-platform compatibility (iOS and Android) and to leverage native device hardware for automated GPS extraction. Simultaneously, the LGU Command Center dashboard is constructed using React and Vite to handle the rapid state updates required for rendering dynamic, live heat maps. The core orchestrator—the backend infrastructure—is engineered using Python FastAPI, chosen specifically for its asynchronous capabilities to manage heavy computational loads, including simultaneous API calls to Google's Gemini servers and the continuous execution of the DBSCAN spatial clustering background processes.
[P108][Normal]: 4. Testing Phase (Verification & Validation): Before deployment, the system undergoes a rigorous Testing Phase to guarantee absolute reliability, security, and algorithmic precision. This phase is critical because the platform directly impacts public safety and municipal resource allocation. Testing is multifaceted, encompassing unit testing of individual code modules, integration testing of the various APIs (Gemini, Google Maps, Firebase), and comprehensive User Acceptance Testing (UAT) with a closed group of residents and LGU dispatchers. The team executes specific stress tests to validate non-functional requirements, such as ensuring the BallTree spatial index can successfully render and search up to 10,000 concurrent geographic data points without perceptible UI latency. Furthermore, the AI triage engine is subjected to rigorous accuracy evaluations; the system must demonstrate that the "Non-Fatal Failure" filter correctly triggers a manual review whenever the Gemini model's classification confidence falls below the strict 75% threshold.
[P109][Normal]: 5. Deployment Phase (Implementation & Rollout): Following successful validation and stakeholder sign-off, the project enters the Deployment Phase. This involves transitioning the software from a local development environment to a secure, live production environment hosted on Google Cloud Run. Because the deployment of a city-wide civic technology platform is complex, it is executed through a controlled, phased rollout strategy known as the 90-Day Predictive Resilience Roadmap. The initial 30 days focus exclusively on asset digitization, where LGU staff utilize the system to map existing infrastructure into the centralized database to form a baseline. This phase also includes comprehensive end-user training, the distribution of User Manuals to LGU dispatchers, and community awareness campaigns to onboard residents. The goal is to ensure a smooth transition from legacy paper-based reporting to the new digital ecosystem without disrupting ongoing municipal operations.
[P110][Normal]: 6. Maintenance Phase (Support & Continuous Improvement): The final phase of the Waterfall Model is the Maintenance Phase, which spans the entire operational lifespan of the deployed system. This phase is dedicated to continuous system monitoring, server optimization, and the resolution of any post-deployment anomalies reported by users. A critical component of this phase for an AI-driven platform is "Model Drift Mitigation." As the physical environment of the city changes and field crews upload verified "Proof of Repair" photographs, these validated images are systematically fed back into a supervised fine-tuning pipeline. This closed-loop learning process ensures that the Gemini Vision model perpetually refines its classification accuracy and severity scoring logic, adapting to the specific visual characteristics of North Caloocan over time. Regular security audits and database backups are also performed to maintain strict compliance with data privacy regulations.

[P158][Normal]: Summary of Responses
[P159][Normal]: Summary of Respondents' Current Reporting Methods
[P161][Normal]: The results show that a concerning majority of respondents (50%) usually report infrastructure problems by posting on Facebook or social media, while a significant portion (35%) do not report the problem at all. Only a small minority (15%) utilize official channels such as going to the Barangay Hall personally or calling the City Hall hotline. This indicates that current official reporting mechanisms possess too much friction, driving citizens toward algorithmic social media noise or fostering total civic apathy.
[P162][Normal]: Summary of Experience with Location Ambiguity
[P164][Normal]: The findings reveal a massive bottleneck in urban dispatch: 85% of respondents find it difficult to explain the exact location of an infrastructure problem to authorities. Specifically, 45% find it "Somewhat difficult – needs landmarks," and 40% find it "Very difficult – no clear address or markers." Only 15% of the surveyed population found explaining their location to be easy. This strongly justifies the necessity of PULSO's automated GPS metadata extraction to eliminate location ambiguity.
[P165][Normal]: Summary of Awareness and Perception of LGU Prioritization
[P167][Normal]: When assessing how authorities prioritize repairs, 55% of residents believe "There is no clear order," while 10% believe repairs only happen "When many people complain." Only 35% felt that dangerous or urgent problems are fixed first. Furthermore, regarding satisfaction with the transparency of the repair process, the community is highly divided, with 35% dissatisfied and 30% remaining neutral. This suggests a profound lack of trust in the current manual triage system, highlighting the need for a transparent, AI-driven prioritization model.
[P168][Normal]: Summary of Respondents' Willingness to Use a Digital Map and AI System
[P170][Normal]: The results show overwhelming support for technological intervention. An extraordinary 95% of respondents stated they would trust a digital map-based report more than a phone call because "the location is clearly shown." Furthermore, all respondents (100%) found the proposed "Snap & Pin" feature helpful and the AI-based predictive alerts useful. Crucially, 100% of the respondents expressed a willingness to use a dedicated mobile app or website for reporting issues (65% answering a definitive "Yes" and 35% answering "Maybe"). This indicates a very high level of acceptance and readiness among citizens to adopt digital solutions for civic reporting, confirming that the PULSO system would be highly beneficial and well-received by the target demographic.

[P174][Normal]: Global Literature on Smart Urban Infrastructure
[P175][Normal]: The global movement towards smart cities is fundamentally driven by the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the Internet of Things (IoT) to foster proactive and resilient urban environments. Recent literature, such as studies from 2025, consistently suggests that transitioning from traditional reactive "break-fix" maintenance models to AI-enabled predictive maintenance (PdM) can yield significant cost reductions, potentially up to 30% in municipal repair expenditures. This research underscores that predictive maintenance extends beyond individual asset management, encompassing entire urban ecosystems, including roads, energy grids, and water systems, to ensure continuous operation and long-term sustainability.
[P176][Normal]: Furthermore, advancements in Geospatial AI (GeoAI) have revolutionized the capture and analysis of complex spatial patterns, which are indispensable for identifying and mitigating urban hazards. Studies emphasize the efficacy of machine learning models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Gemini and GPT-4o, in categorizing infrastructure defects from visual data with reported accuracies exceeding 92%. This level of automation is particularly crucial in regions often referred to as the "Global South" and in rapidly transitioning cities, where institutional inertia and fragmented governance structures frequently impede timely and critical infrastructure interventions.
[P177][Normal]: To further enhance this understanding, additional international literature highlights the growing importance of digital twins in urban planning and infrastructure management. For instance, research by Kitchin (2020) on "The Data Revolution: Big Data, Open Data, Data Infrastructures and their Consequences" emphasizes how comprehensive data collection and modeling, foundational to digital twins, can provide real-time insights into urban systems, enabling more informed decision-making and predictive capabilities. Similarly, Batty (2018) in "The New Science of Cities" discusses the theoretical underpinnings of urban complexity and the role of computational models in understanding and managing urban growth and infrastructure, providing a broader context for PULSO's data-driven approach. The concept of citizen science in urban monitoring is also gaining traction, with studies by Haklay (2013) on "Citizen Science and Volunteered Geographic Information: Opportunities and Challenges" demonstrating how public participation can augment official data collection, aligning with PULSO's citizen-centric feedback mechanism.

This fragmentation, coupled with a lack of centralized tracking mechanisms, severely hinders Local Government Units (LGUs) from making data-driven decisions and effectively managing public assets. This "Visibility Gap" is further compounded by what has been termed the "social media governance fallacy," where citizens frequently resort to informal platforms like Facebook for reporting issues, which inherently lack the geospatial precision and structured data required for a professional engineering work order lifecycle.

This national literature unequivocally underscores the urgent necessity for localized platforms like PULSO. [P195][Normal]: Software Requirements Specifications (SRS)
[P196][Normal]: System Features
[P197][Normal]: The PULSO platform is designed as a multi-tier, highly integrated ecosystem consisting of a Citizen Mobile Application developed in Flutter, a Local Government Unit (LGU) Command Center dashboard developed in React/Vite, and an AI-driven Backend architecture powered by Python FastAPI.
[P199][Normal]: Functional Requirements
[P200][Normal]: Multimodal Report Submission: The system shall allow residents to easily upload photographic evidence and text descriptions of utility issues (e.g., broken streetlights, flooded roads).
[P201][Normal]: Automated Geospatial Tagging: The mobile client shall automatically capture, extract, and attach precise GPS coordinates (Latitude and Longitude) to every report without requiring manual user input.
[P202][Normal]: Gemini AI Surgical Triage: The system shall integrate the Gemini 2.5 Flash model to instantly analyze uploaded images, classify the specific utility type, and assign a standardized Severity Score from 1 to 10 based on the detected hazard level.
[P203][Normal]: Geospatial Deduplication (GDM): The system backend shall utilize the DBSCAN spatial clustering algorithm to detect multiple reports originating from the same geographic vicinity and merge them into a single, actionable Incident Ticket.
[P204][Normal]: Real-Time Operational Heat Map: The LGU Command Center dashboard shall visually render incident clusters using dynamic, color-coded intensity layers to highlight critical operational "Red Zones."
[P205][Normal]: Enterprise Asset Management (EAM): The system shall allow administrators to track the lifecycle of a report through standardized statuses: Pending, AI Verified, Dispatched, In-Progress, and Resolved.
[P206][Normal]: Automated Status Loop: The system shall automatically broadcast real-time push notifications to the reporting citizen whenever the status of their submitted ticket changes.
[P207][Normal]: Offline Synchronization: The mobile application shall feature an offline mode allowing residents in cellular dead zones to save drafts, which will automatically sync to the server once network connectivity is restored.
[P209][Normal]: Non - Functional Requirements
[P210][Normal]: Usability: The user interfaces for both the mobile application and the web dashboard shall strictly adhere to Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.1) to ensure unhindered usability for elderly citizens and those with visual impairments.
[P211][Normal]: Performance: The Gemini AI multimodal triage process, including image analysis and JSON generation, must complete within a latency window of 3 to 5 seconds to provide immediate psychological reassurance to the user.
[P212][Normal]: Security: The system shall enforce JSON Web Tokens (JWT) for authentication. All data in transit must be encrypted using Transport Layer Security (TLS 1.3), and all data at rest shall utilize AES-256 encryption to protect citizen identifiers.
[P213][Normal]: Availability: The cloud-hosted infrastructure must guarantee 99.9% uptime, ensuring the platform remains accessible during severe weather events when reporting is most critical.
[P214][Normal]: Reliability: The spatial database must maintain transactional integrity (ACID compliance) to ensure no citizen reports are lost or duplicated during concurrent high-volume submissions.
[P215][Normal]: Scalability: The backend spatial indexing architecture must support the rendering and clustering of up to 10,000 concurrent geographic data points without perceptible UI lag.
[P216][Normal]: Interfaces
[P217][Normal]: Citizen Interface (Mobile): A mobile-first, highly intuitive "Snap & Pin" interface designed for maximum friction reduction during the reporting process.
[P218][Normal]: LGU Dispatcher Interface (Web): A comprehensive GIS-enabled dashboard featuring interactive heat maps, ticket queue management, and analytics modules.
[P219][Normal]: Field Crew Interface (Mobile/Tablet): A streamlined digital job ticket interface displaying exact coordinates, hazard details, and a module to upload "Proof of Repair" photographs.
[P220][Normal]: Constraints
[P221][Normal]: Technical Constraints
[P222][Normal]: The system must be developed utilizing React for the frontend and Python for the API-based backend.
[P223][Normal]: Geospatial queries and deduplication logic must utilize MySQL 8.0 configured with Spatial Reference System Identifier (SRID) 4326 to handle geographic ellipsoid mathematics accurately.
[P224][Normal]: The AI integration is strictly constrained to using the Gemini 2.5 Flash API with forced structured JSON outputs.
[P225][Normal]: The mobile application relies on the accuracy of the host device's built-in GPS hardware; deep urban canyons may occasionally skew coordinate precision.
[P226][Normal]: Business Constraints
[P227][Normal]: The system must be hosted within budget parameters acceptable for a municipal LGU, utilizing efficient pay-as-you-go cloud architectures.
[P228][Normal]: The deployment must align with the operational capacities of the Caloocan City North Engineering Department.
[P229][Normal]: Acceptance Criteria
[P230][Normal]: The system will be evaluated based on the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality models. A "Pass" condition requires that the DBSCAN algorithm successfully groups 95% of test reports within a 50-meter radius into a single cluster. The Gemini AI must maintain a classification accuracy rate exceeding 85% across a standardized dataset of urban utility hazards. The platform must also pass a comprehensive Data Privacy Act compliance audit, successfully sanitizing all personally identifiable information (PII) from the public-facing heat map before deployment.
[P232][Normal]: System Design Phase (System Planning & Modeling)
[P233][Normal]: System Design Document
[P234][Normal]: The System Design Document (SDD) outlines a highly modular, cloud-native architecture. The system decouples the client-side data intake from the heavy computational processing of the AI and spatial clustering engines. The Python FastAPI backend acts as the central orchestrator, receiving RESTful payloads from the Flutter mobile app, routing images to the Gemini API, calculating spatial proximities, and pushing structured results to the MySQL geodatabase.
[P235][Normal]: Data Flow Diagram
[P236][Normal]: The Data Flow Diagram (DFD) maps the lifecycle of a citizen report.
[P237][Normal]: Input: A citizen captures an image and submits it via the mobile app. The device injects GPS metadata.
[P238][Normal]: Triage Process: The payload is sent to the backend. The backend queries the Gemini 2.5 Flash API. Gemini returns a JSON object with the classification and severity score.
[P239][Normal]: Deduplication Process: The backend runs the DBSCAN algorithm against the existing database to determine if the new report belongs to an existing cluster.
[P240][Normal]: Storage: The validated, clustered data is saved to the MySQL database.
[P241][Normal]: Output: The React dashboard queries the database and renders the updated Operational Heat Map. Push notifications are triggered via Firebase back to the citizen.
[P242][Normal]: Technology Selection
[P243][Normal]: Frontend (Mobile): Flutter was selected for its ability to compile natively to both iOS and Android from a single codebase, drastically reducing development overhead.
[P244][Normal]: Frontend (Web): React and Vite were chosen for the LGU dashboard due to their superior performance in rendering complex, dynamic UI components and rapid state updates required for live map tracking.
[P245][Normal]: Backend: Python FastAPI was selected over traditional frameworks (like Django) for its asynchronous capabilities and inherent speed, which are critical when managing thousands of concurrent spatial queries and AI API calls.
[P246][Normal]: AI Engine: Gemini 2.5 Flash was chosen over competitors due to its massive context window, exceptional speed-to-cost ratio, and native, robust support for strictly structured JSON outputs.

[P287][Normal]: 90-Day Predictive Resilience Roadmap
[P288][Normal]: Phase 1 (Days 1-30): Asset Digitization. The initial month focuses on establishing a structural baseline. All existing LGU infrastructure is mapped into the SRID 4326 geodatabase.
[P289][Normal]: Phase 2 (Days 31-60): The Preventive Transition. The AI models begin analyzing incoming report clusters, identifying assets suffering from statistically abnormal failure rates (MTBF) to initiate early interventions.
[P290][Normal]: Phase 3 (Days 61-90): Predictive Mastery. The system integrates historical degradation data to generate Remaining Useful Life (RUL) estimates, permanently transitioning the LGU from reactive responses to predictive urban resilience.
[P292][Normal]: Maintenance Phase
[P293][Normal]: Maintenance Reports
[P294][Normal]: Post-deployment, the system requires continuous monitoring to ensure high availability and accuracy. A critical maintenance activity involves "AI Model Drift Mitigation." As field crews upload "Proof of Repair" photographs, these validated images are fed back into a supervised fine-tuning pipeline for the Gemini Vision model. This closed-loop learning process ensures that the AI's classification accuracy and severity scoring logic perpetually adapt to the specific visual characteristics of North Caloocan's infrastructure over time.
